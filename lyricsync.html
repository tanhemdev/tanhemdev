Technical Architecture

LyricSync follows what you play on Spotify (PKCE sign-in, read-only), fetches time-synced lyrics from LRCLIB, and translates each line for meaning with Claude, through a small serverless function that keeps the API key off the browser. A playback clock smooths Spotify's polling so the highlight stays on the beat. A free Chrome extension does the same for the Spotify and Apple Music web players, translating on your own computer with Chrome's built-in translator. Poetry stays poetry. Slang stays slang.

The Problem

128 million people watched the Super Bowl halftime. Most had no idea what he was saying.

Bad Bunny's Super Bowl LX halftime show was the first performed almost entirely in Spanish, and an estimated 128.2 million people watched it (Nielsen, via Billboard). Most of his listeners don't speak Spanish, and word-for-word translation doesn't help: it turns pana (buddy) into "corduroy" and sin pena (no holding back) into "without pain." The music hits. The meaning doesn't.

Where It Stands

From a Super Bowl question to a working app.

The demo above runs the full pipeline on two original songs. To follow your own music for free, the LyricSync Chrome extension works with the Spotify and Apple Music web players: try DtMF by Bad Bunny, FAKE LOVE by BTS or Tum Hi Ho by Arijit Singh. Lyrics load live from LRCLIB and translation runs on your computer.
